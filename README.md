VICE COVER turns your photos and a caption into GTA 6-style poster cover art: multi-panel layouts (Vice Trio, Split Duo, Solo Hero, a five-panel Cover Grid with one hero panel and four satellites, Cast Cover — an asymmetric four-panel showcase built around one dominant hero panel whose face stands at poster scale, two stacked portrait panels and a full-height scene panel, every panel captioned with its cast role — and Mosaic — a nine-panel 3×3 grid in the classic cover pattern whose lettering sits in a title band below the grid, so all nine panels stay fully visible), a sunset-gradient title with a double outline (a polished chrome gradient in Noir; on Blockbuster the sunset family itself — cream into gold into orange — with a dark plum rim and a hard extruded block of maroon-plum shade stepped under the fill, so the lettering sits in the art the way the winning covers' does), a neon script tagline, and seven colour grades (Sunset, Blockbuster — the photographic class: a continuous 256-step gradient map with no posterize at all, so every strand of hair, skin texture and sparkle of sea survives, enriched by a gentle S-curve and a saturation scale around each pixel's own luminance that pushes the sunset deep and warm while skin stays protected — a skin-tone detector moderates the grade's mix and saturation exactly where skin sits, so faces never go tangerine; detected skin whose hue still sits in the magenta sector is rotated toward the warm brown family with its saturation and value preserved, so a magenta-lit face lands in the same colour family as the field's best covers instead of glowing pink; the sun renders in this grade as a soft photographic glow melted into the panel's own light, never a hard vector disc; and in multi-panel layouts each satellite panel's white balance is walked toward the hero panel's — measured per panel from its graded colours, skin exempt from the walk — so the whole grid reads as one world under one sunset — Neon, Palm Gold, Noir, Poster Ink — a bold screen-print duotone with a firmer posterize and a jittered halftone dot screen that lives in smooth highlights — sky, sea, glint — and fades out over detailed texture (fabric, hair) and off bright neutral surfaces like white clothing entirely, never on faces — and Key Art, the painted class described below), all but Key Art and Blockbuster applied as true gradient maps with a light cel-shade posterize. An optional poster dressing — the furniture of a real one-sheet, drawn procedurally into the poster and therefore into every export while it is on — adds a tilted starburst seal, a five-star review line with a pull-quote above the title, a caption chip on every panel of the Cover Grid (a place, a time, a story beat) — on Cast Cover the chips name each panel's cast role over the same place-and-time line, a banner strip along the foot with drawn palm silhouettes, a spaced-capital slogan, an EST. 2026 chip and a drawn barcode block, and a one-line credit microtext at the very edge. Noir and Poster Ink carry shadow protection: through the shadows their ramps cross-fade to an un-posterized gradient map, so a face sitting in shade — or a whole photo taken at night — keeps its features instead of collapsing into one flat tone; Noir additionally runs an ordered Bayer dither through its posterize, breaking the flat grey plateaus a B/W quantization leaves on faces into a fine print texture that restores their tonal modelling. Key Art paints instead of grading: the photo is first softened on an edge-aware base (repeated downscale–upscale rounds dissolve photographic micro-detail and skin texture while the big shapes and hard contours survive), then mapped through a warm sunset-family ramp posterized to eight cel bands whose transitions are smoothstepped — colour areas read as flat, graded brush strokes — with shadow protection like Noir's, and finished with drawn ink contours: a Sobel pass over the softened base, computed at quarter resolution, inks the strong edges (hair and jaw silhouettes, hat brims, clothing folds, palm fronds, the horizon) in a warm dark plum-brown in two coats — a soft coat whose alpha follows edge strength, plus a second, near-full-strength coat reserved for the strongest contours alone — so the outlines read as drawn at full-frame scale while faces — whose shading gradients sit far below both ink thresholds — stay clean, under a fine paper grain; even the sun is repainted in this grade — a warm painted disc with soft shadow bands and a thin ink outline in the contour family, not the hard-slit clip-art disc of the graded classes — and the ramp's low end leans violet-to-magenta, so painted shadows keep their Vice purple depth. An automatic exposure lift steadies dark photos before grading (measured on the photo's mean luminance; bright photos pass through completely untouched, genuinely very dark photos get a substantially stronger lift whose highlight shoulder keeps lit neon signs separated instead of blown out, and night shots still look like night). Where a layout has more panels than loaded photos, a photo's later showings are mirrored and re-cropped, so a repeat reads as a different moment of the same shoot rather than a copy-paste. Nine bundled sample photos (three bright Vice scenes, a neon night portrait, a marina portrait in a red dress, a surfer carrying her board at golden hour, an aerial causeway with cars crossing turquoise water, a vintage service station under a neon GAS sign at dusk and a 1960 Impala convertible crossing a city intersection at dusk) show the range; drop in any photos of your own, drag to crop each panel, pick a post size (square, portrait or landscape), and export a share-ready PNG — one size, or all three sizes at once. Everything runs locally in the browser — your photos never leave your machine, and the app works fully offline with no network calls and no Rockstar artwork, logos or fonts.

Open `app/index.html` in a browser to use it.

## Run it

```sh
node main.ts
```

`main.ts` (Node 22, standard library only, no network) writes the submission dossier for the entry.

Round 12 in brief: the Cover Grid showcase mixes portraits with these two cinematic scenes (the causeway and the neon street take the satellite panels that used to hold the convertible driver and the night portrait — both still bundled samples, one tap away in the editor); the dressed Grid's title became a bottom band fitted to two-thirds of the frame width, in the winning covers' pattern; Blockbuster's skin taming was strengthened (wider detector gates, deeper mix/saturation floors) until the pier fisherman's measured skin saturation fell clearly below its round-11 value while the heroine's stayed in its passed class; the starburst seal moved off the gutter — where a spike touched the fisherman's shoulder — onto the causeway panel's open sea; and the banner strip's centre line now speaks a second beat ("FIVE FACES — ONE COAST — NO WAY BACK") instead of repeating the script tagline.

Round 13 in brief: the round-12 band had buried the neon street (only a dark strip of it showed) and cut the heroine at the neck, leaving a floating head. The two scene panels traded cells — the neon street now takes the top-right cell, the one satellite the lettering band never reaches, so the whole scene reads; the causeway scenery takes the bottom-left cell behind the band, the backdrop role scenery plays on the winning covers. The hero shot is reframed from a tight crop to a mid shot: her head, shoulders and chest stay above the title's top edge and the letters ride her car and belt line instead of her neck. The script tagline is fitted narrower and pulled left until its measured ink and glow clear every subject on the cover. The banner line is corrected to count what the cover actually shows ("ONE COAST — FIVE STORIES — NO WAY BACK" — three faces, two scenes, five panels). Blockbuster gains the magenta hue tame described above: measured on the export, the fisherman's cheek moved from hue ~343 to ~11 and his beard from ~349 to ~9, at essentially unchanged saturation, while the heroine's and the marina subject's skin measured unmoved. The seal shrank by about a fifth; a pixel scan proved the causeway cell has no clean sky at the seal's radius (its visible strip is all palm crown and trunk), so the seal moved to the hero panel's sky, measured clean — pure sky, clear of both palm trunks, the chip, the review pill and her hair.

Round 14 in brief: one defect, measured before it was touched. The fisherman's beard is grey-white in the source photo (saturation 0.12–0.19), a class the skin detector barely sees, so the grade's full ramp and saturation enrich painted it freely; sampling the round-13 export showed the surviving pink was not his skin (his cheek already measured in the natural band) but a fringe population in the beard at hue 350–360 and 0–10 with saturation 0.34–0.52 — the magenta tame's protective fade-out at 350–356 degrees, kept for the marina subject's cheek, let exactly that fringe through. Blockbuster therefore gains a fringe tame beside the magenta tame: pixels in that hue sector, in a moderate saturation band, at sunlit values, with blue level with green (the pink relation — sunset orange fails it), are rotated the short arc toward 16 degrees with their chroma scaled down — and, the decisive gate, only where the pixel fringes on near-neutral source neighbours, the texture of stubble itself. The marina cheek matches the beard on every per-pixel test but is a smooth field with no neutral neighbours, and it measures pixel-for-pixel unmoved (patch mean identical, hue 358.5 before and after), as does the heroine's jaw. Measured on the exports: the beard's pink-sector share in the moderate saturation band fell from 0.38 to 0.18 (Grid) and 0.27 to 0.12 (Duo), its 350–360 hue share from 0.21 to 0.12 and 0.13 to 0.07, the moustache patch's vivid-pink share from 0.61 to 0.00, and his cheek holds the natural band at hue 12.7, saturation 0.40. What remains, honestly: a pale warm wash in the lit stubble at crop zoom — the beard reads grey-brown and sunlit rather than coral-fringed, and the patch straddling his lip line keeps the lip's own red.

Round 15 in brief: one defect, again measured before it was touched. The review pill in the landscape Grid was filled translucent (0.62 alpha), and at native resolution the hero photo's palm crowns silhouetted through its body — the pill was not sitting in dead space, it was washing over the crowns. The honest fix would be dead space, so the undressed hero panel was scanned for a clean window at the pill's size: 4,253 placements inside the panel (clear of the chip and the seal), and not one measured clean — the best still held 10.0% palm-and-hair-dark pixels, and shrinking the pill to 0.85 of its size (6,644 placements) still left 6.0%, because crowns, two trunks and the heroine's hair cross every strip of that sky. With no clean window to move to, that one pill is now filled fully opaque — the same solid the seal and chips already print — scoped to the landscape Grid only, so no other layout's rendering changes by a byte. The crowns behind it stay behind it; nothing shows through.

Round 16 in brief: the two scene samples were the cover's weakest panels — the neon street's crowd melted into an unreadable blob at panel size and the causeway was nearly an empty palm silhouette — so both were replaced with CC0 scenes built around one unmissable subject, the pattern the field's best covers use: a sports car sharp under neon signage for the NEON DISTRICT panel, and a lifeguard tower with the sun blazing through its cabin for the PALMERA CAUSEWAY panel, reframed (zoomed, panned, bottom-ridden) so the strip of its cell that clears the title band holds the tower's sunlit cabin and slats, and its caption chip lands on the open sand between the tower's legs — verified by crop, clear of the tower, the distant walkers and every palm, exactly as the neon panel's chip was verified over dead night sky. The title gained the depth it was missing: Blockbuster lettering now stands on an extruded block of shade — six solid impressions of the glyphs stepped down and to the right in a deep maroon-plum, deep enough to clear the rim stroke that follows them (a shallower first attempt measured pixel-identical to round 15: the rim had swallowed it whole) — while its fit, centre and baseline never moved, no face is touched, and the script rides the extrusion the way the winners' scripts ride theirs. Nothing else moved: the seal, pill, chips and banner keep their round-15 places, the grade's skin tames are untouched (the three control skins measure hue-identical to round 15), and the Noir cover renders byte-for-byte as before.

Round 18 in brief: two rounds of judging converged on the same lesson — the causeway panel's problem was its photograph, not its place: the lifeguard tower read as empty scenery whether the title band swallowed it or the panel stood clear, and the neon street's Japanese signage read off-world for a Leonida cover. Both scene samples are replaced again, content first, with the grid's geometry untouched. The causeway is now an aerial of a Miami causeway — a bridge deck carrying cars (one of them red) over turquoise water, boats and a wake below, the skyline behind — reframed so the strip of its cell that clears the title band holds the deck, its cars and the open water, and its caption chip lands on the bay's open water, verified by crop, clear of the deck, the boats and the shore tree. The neon panel is now a vintage American service station at dusk, its tall red GAS blade sign and neon roofline lit over the pumps — the neon gas-station beat the winning covers play — framed so the sign reads whole and the panel's chip hangs on pure dusk sky. Nothing else moved: the portraits, the grade's skin tames, the extruded title, the seal, pill and banner all keep their round-16 places.

Round 21 in brief: a full sweep of the CC0 pool (round 20) proved no downloadable photograph beats the bundled heroine shot on face, convertible and sunset together — every molten-sky candidate lost the face — so the hero sample itself was re-graded at the file level instead: a sky-only edit of `app/samples/boulevard.jpg`. The sky was mapped from pixel sampling (pale gold: hue 23–47°, saturation 0.13–0.38) and intensified inside a feathered colour-and-proximity mask — the mask follows the sky's own colour gate (bright, warm, unsaturated), blurred wide, suppressed near every foreground edge and over a dedicated skin detector, and re-checked against the raw gate per pixel, so hair, skin, clothing and the car receive exactly zero edit. Inside the mask the hue is walked along a vertical molten gradient (magenta-pink at the top of the frame through red to orange at the horizon, golden again inside the sun's glow) and saturation is scaled up roughly two-to-threefold: measured on the edited file, the sky's mean saturation rose from 0.13/0.29/0.38 (top/mid/low bands) to 0.46/0.54/0.54, with band hues landing at 0°/21°/32°. Safety was proven, not assumed: skin patches (cheek, forehead, neck, hand) measure hue-identical within 0.7° and saturation within 0.002; 28 samples along the hairline moved at most 3.9° with zero new magenta pixels in the hair; and outside the mask the mean per-channel difference is 1.2 (re-encode noise). The render pipeline is untouched — no grade, geometry, dressing or framing changed this round.

Round 22 in brief: the repair round for round 21's two measured failures. (1) Skin versus the white-balance walk: the harmonize walk exempts skin through a graded-pixel shield, but gate-by-gate measurement of the walked skin on the pier panel blamed two gates — the red-blue ramp (pink-lit and shaded skin rides blue high, so its red-blue gap fell under the ramp's foot: 10,901 of the chest's 11,424 walked skin pixels) and the brightness fade (sunlit skin above 0.92 lost its shield: 3,914 on the face). The unshielded minority sat interleaved with exempt skin under the panel's strongest walk, and that interleave printed as a pink-magenta mottle on the fisherman's face, neck and chest in the Duo portrait. The shield is now the maximum of the original test and two companions sharing its outer gates — a shaded/pink-lit test (red-blue ramp from 0.015, brightness fade at 0.94–0.995) and a sunlit test (no brightness fade) — and measured on the same pixels the walked skin falls from 5.0/6.1/13.6% (face/neck/chest) to 0.0/0.0/0.03%, and on the Grid's pier panel from 10.2% to 0.01%. Non-skin keeps walking and the gains themselves are computed from un-walked renders, so the cover's colour unity is unchanged. (2) The molten sky, rebuilt from the round-19 original of `app/samples/boulevard.jpg` rather than stacked on round 21's edit: round 21's sky read as a flat artificial wash with coarse stepped banding in the top band. The new edit maps hue and saturation through smooth continuous functions of vertical position (a cosine-interpolated molten profile, no discrete steps), adds fine photographic grain (σ ≈ 1.2 levels, luminance-led) inside the sky mask only, to break the banding the grade's tonal steps would otherwise print, and cools the top band by half a step from round 21 — measured on a fixed sky mask, the top band keeps 44% of round 21's hue shift and 46% of its saturation shift, while the sun zone measures identical to round 21 (hue 23.7°, saturation 0.401 against 23.3°/0.403) and the horizon is untouched. Protection is round 21's method, hardened where measurement demanded it: the warm sector is tight (the heroine's mauve shirt shadow at hue ~335° passed a wide sector and took a salmon smear in an early pass — the sector now ends at 44° from the warm centre, and a paleness gate calibrated on the sunlit shoulder ridge, saturation 0.13–0.16 against the true glow's 0.42, excludes bright cloth outright). Final numbers: 29 hairline samples move at most 3.5° with zero new magenta; face/neck skin averages +0.03° of hue; the shirt block differs from the original by 0.70 mean (re-encode noise, cleaner than round 21's own 0.79); outside the mask the mean difference is 1.00. Nothing else moved: geometry, title, dressing, the other samples and every other grade path render as round 21 left them.

Round 34 in brief: the vehicle panel every recent judging round found missing. The Blockbuster Grid showcase's weakest cell — the causeway aerial in the bottom-left satellite, most of it buried under the title band, reading as a dark filler strip — trades its photograph for the bundled cruiser (round 26's 1960 Impala street scene, restored above), and the combination no earlier round tested is the one measured here: this photo, in the causeway's cell, under round 32's selective bold grade at the full dose factor 1 (in round 26 it ran in the marina's cell under the base grade and lost narrowly to the marina portrait; in rounds 24–25 the cell's classic-car beat was a front clip that read as a fender macro). The photo is framed for this cell (zoom 1.15, ox 0.49, oy −0.60: window at photo x 0.03–0.90, y 0.25–0.94), so the whole car — nose, windshield with its two small distant occupants, tail fin, wheels — lands at cell y 0.24–0.60 in the strip that clears the lettering, and the panel's chip takes round 26's text for this photo (OCEAN DRIVE — 5:12 PM — TOP DOWN, still literally true of it) and rides the asphalt under the car's tail, clear of the body. Nothing else moved, measured on the lossless exports: every differing pixel of the landscape cover (122,869) sits inside the replaced cell's polygon — six of them on the shared divider's anti-aliased edge — the hero, pier, GAS and marina panels measure zero differing pixels in their own territory, the footer band outside the cell is untouched, and the Duo portrait render is pixel-identical to round 32. Colour, honestly: the bold dose is not free on this hazy source — the car box measures mean saturation 0.652 and a magenta-sector share of 30.5%, the bold ramp's plum claiming the shaded street and chrome harder than round 26's base grade claimed them (its lamp/chrome boxes then measured 11.6–13.1%), the silver paint warming to champagne-pink; the panel reads as the cover's darkest, plumdest satellite, in the same family as the GAS panel's night. The two faces aboard stay small and distant — no close face enters the cover — and the grade's skin shields run inside the dosed render as everywhere else.
